# Methodological note

Read this before comparing country scores, changing normalization, or publishing a ranking.

## Research population and time

The universe consists of the 193 UN members, the Holy See and Palestine as observers, and Taiwan and Kosovo as additional entities. Membership status remains a column; the web page defaults to UN members. Kosovo uses the user-assigned XK/XKX convention, not an officially assigned ISO 3166-1 code. Inclusion does not express a position on political status. Palestine joins to the World Bank's West Bank and Gaza series; this territorial definition must be considered when combining sources. World Bank region labels are used without reclassification.

The study uses the last twelve **complete calendar months** before the collection date in Europe/Prague. On 16 October 2026, that means 1 October 2025–30 September 2026. The current month is omitted. Google request geography is worldwide (`geo=""`), category is all categories (`0`), and property is Google Web Search (`""`). Source dates and retrieval times are recorded separately.

## Search construct

The target is interest in a country topic as classified by Google, across search origins. It is not search activity originating in that country, the country's entire cultural footprint, or every query mentioning one of its places, citizens or products. Country topics reduce language and homonym problems but cannot eliminate classification errors. Review the selected entity, its type and related searches. Reject a football team, US state, person, bird or food entity even when its label resembles the country's name.

Google's [normalization explanation](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en) describes relative interest and low-volume suppression. Let `q_it` denote the topic's share of Google searches worldwide in interval `t`. For batch `b`, the observed value is approximately `y_bit = c_b q_it`, rounded and potentially suppressed. The batch constant changes between independently exported comparisons. Thus a 60 from one export is not comparable to a 30 from another without calibration.

## Shared-reference calibration

The implemented estimator uses shared topic/interval observations. All batches must have the same source window, interval resolution, study-calendar interpretation, origin geography, category and search property. Google documents UTC for Explore periods of 30 days or longer, including this yearly window. Europe/Prague determines which twelve complete calendar months to request; it is not the source time zone. The date-only CSV contains no offset and no sub-day conversion is inferred. Original import manifests retain their historical `timezone` value as a study-calendar field; provenance records source UTC separately. A country must retain one topic ID. Duplicate rows and conflicting topic identities fail validation.

For two batches sharing topics, estimate `d_ab = median(log(y_ait) - log(y_bit))` over identical intervals where both values are at least 5. Require six usable overlapping topic-interval observations. Six is a pragmatic validation threshold, not a statistical guarantee. The 90th percentile absolute log deviation must not exceed 0.2. This corresponds to approximately 22% multiplicative disagreement and is a quality gate, not a confidence interval.

Solve weighted least squares for `log(k_b) - log(k_a) = d_ab`, weighting each batch link by its usable overlap count and fixing one root scale. All batches must connect to the reference country. Reject a disconnected graph or a fitted link residual above 0.2. Linked weaker country topics can bridge comparisons that would round small countries to zero beside a large reference. The current implementation requires those bridge exports to be supplied; it does not construct an anchor bank automatically.

Multiply each observation by its fitted batch scale. Shared topics are repeated measurements, never additional traffic. For a country/interval present more than once, retain the observation with the highest original score to reduce integer-rounding error; alphabetical batch ID breaks ties. This can introduce selection effects and is not an uncertainty estimate. Repeated-export stability analysis and alternative reference paths are required before publication as a validated ranking. The deterministic checks described below are now part of every build, but do not establish sampling stability. The procedure is inspired by [G-TAB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13861), but is deliberately simpler. Its empirical measurement accuracy and sampling stability have not been independently established.

## Monthly and annual indices

Monthly exports are used directly. Daily or weekly interval means are weighted by the number of covered calendar days in each month. Splitting a week across months assumes constant search share within that week, so the output is a monthly approximation. Partial or missing coverage is not extrapolated. A month containing a suppressed value (`0` or `<1`) stays missing, rather than acquiring an invented zero-search count.

Scale the reference country's mean over the twelve complete months to 100. Define `S_i* = (1/12) Σ_t I_it`, the equal-month mean of the calibrated relative search-share series. This is an annual **relative attention index**, not a sum of searches. It weights calendar months equally; daily weights are used within months. Search totals vary over time, and their monthly denominators are unknown, so this cannot recover annual query counts. A choice to weight months by days would define a different index.

The principal per-capita measure is `A_i* = 10^6 S_i* / P_i`, labeled **relative attention index per million residents**. Values depend on the reference scale; ordering does not depend on an arbitrary common multiplier. Country topics may overlap, so country indices are not assumed to sum to a share of all searches about countries.

Rankings require all twelve monthly values, no suppressed intervals and no accepted interval with an original score below 5. This conservative rule can exclude small countries and bias the measured subset. Their status is retained and they are not interpreted as the least-searched countries. A country without population may have a total relative index but has no per-capita rank. Ties share the minimum rank. Top/bottom lists contain up to twenty eligible entities; at fewer than forty measured entities they can overlap, and always refer only to the measured subset.

The web top/bottom lists can be filtered by inclusive minimum and maximum population. Their histogram counts eligible countries in logarithmically spaced population ranges; excluded portions are faded. Both lists recalculate ranks within the selected range. The page summary, other charts, full-data rankings and economic model retain the chosen country scope and are not changed by this population filter. The two range handles cannot cross, and an empty selection is reported without assigning ranks.

## Foreign attention

`(S_i - D_i)/P_i` requires compatible worldwide and domestic counts, or documented weights that transform origin-specific measures into the same additive count basis. Regional Trends popularity indices do not satisfy that condition. Even an API with consistent scaling does not establish regional additivity. The project keeps absolute counts and foreign attention missing; a guarded calculation helper rejects regional-index input. No regional subtraction is performed.

## Economic denominators and model

Use World Bank indicators `SP.POP.TOTL`, `NY.GDP.MKTP.CD`, `NY.GDP.PCAP.CD` and optional `ST.INT.ARVL`, source 2. For each indicator/entity, select the latest positive observation in the six-year interval ending at the fixed economic reference year, defaulting to the year before the search window ends. Save every observation year and the full downloaded history. Population is not projected forward to the search year. This creates a timing limitation, especially for fast-changing or displaced populations.

The web scatterplot can compare total relative search attention with population, total GDP, or GDP per capita. The GDP-per-capita view uses the cached World Bank `NY.GDP.PCAP.CD` observation and its year, following the same six-year selection rule above; it does not divide independently selected GDP and population values. GDP per capita measures economic output per person in current US dollars, not personal income or purchasing-power-adjusted output. Only ranking-eligible countries with positive values on both axes are plotted. The line and R² are a separate descriptive log-log fit for the countries shown, with no population control and no causal interpretation. Switching chart views does not change the population-and-total-GDP model or the rankings.

Total GDP combines population size and output per person, so its strong association with search attention should not be read as a relationship with prosperity alone. A weaker GDP-per-capita-only fit does not establish that prosperity is unimportant after accounting for population. For the matched-year model inputs, write `GDP = P × GDPpc`: the existing model is equivalently `log(S_i*) = α + (β + γ) log(P_i) + γ log(GDPpc_i) + ε_i`. Thus, at equal population, doubling GDP per capita corresponds to a fitted attention ratio of `2^γ` (about 1.6 in this snapshot). The interface derives this ratio from the saved coefficient and labels it as an association in the full model sample. This interpretation uses same-year GDP divided by population, rather than the independently selected World Bank per-capita series in the standalone chart. Predictions, residuals and rankings are unchanged; neither fit establishes causation.

For Taiwan and the Holy See, which lack World Bank population, this snapshot now uses two explicitly reviewed official supplements. This extends the original World Bank-only denominator policy; it does not change any search-quality rule.

The rebuild verifies source checksums, reviewed status, unique known entities, positive integer counts and dates inside the same six-year economic window. A supplement cannot replace an existing World Bank population. `population_source`, `population_source_url`, `population_as_of`, `population_definition` and `population_note` accompany the country exports, and the full records enter provenance. GDP remains missing for these two entities, so they do not enter the population-and-GDP regression.

Fit `log(S_i*) = α + β log(P_i) + γ log(GDP_i) + ε_i` using at least ten eligible countries. Select the latest common year with positive World Bank population and GDP, within the economic reference year and the two preceding years. This selection uses the full cached economic history, not the independently latest values shown in the country table. Save `model_year`, `model_population`, `model_gdp_usd` and `model_status` alongside each country. The per-capita denominator remains unchanged. Selecting the latest indicators independently and then rejecting year mismatches needlessly excluded seven countries in the original model. The revised snapshot includes 186 model countries instead of 179, including Monaco, San Marino and Liechtenstein; the other four additions are Afghanistan, Bahamas, Lebanon and the United Arab Emirates. Report exclusions, sample size, coefficients, R², HC3 standard errors/95% intervals, condition number, leverage and Cook's distance. R² describes the share of sample variance in log attention captured by the fitted model; it is neither the fraction of raw search volume explained nor an out-of-sample prediction score. Population and total GDP can be correlated; coefficient interpretations are conditional and should not be treated as causal.

The residual is `R_i = log(S_i*) - fitted_log(S_i*)`; `exp(R_i)` is attention relative to the fitted geometric prediction. This is not an arithmetic conditional mean or a predicted count. Changing the overall search-index scale changes only the intercept. Compare adjusted and per-capita ranks using Spearman correlation. Other correlations, region distributions and tourism comparisons are descriptive; observation years can differ and missing tourism coverage is substantial. No significance-based causal claim is made.

Indicator correlations require observations no more than two years before the economic reference year. All available tourism observations in the delivered snapshot are from 2020 and are therefore excluded from these correlations. They remain available as historical context in the exports.

Inspect the association of population with per-capita attention, remembering that population occurs in the denominator. Save each country's peak-month share and recompute per-capita ranks after removing its highest month. This is an event-sensitivity diagnostic: it does not identify a news event. External contemporaneous evidence is needed before attributing a spike to a particular event.

## Limits on publication

The delivered panel contains 76 native Google Trends comparison exports covering all 197 entities and publishes per-capita ranks for 194 eligible entities, including the two official population supplements. The country identities were reviewed and the full comparison graph passes calibration checks. Tests on synthetic fixtures establish implementation behavior; they do not establish sampling stability or empirical ranking accuracy. Cabo Verde, Comoros and Haiti remain excluded for low integer precision. Before publication, audit topics and territorial coverage, collect repeated samples, examine alternate anchors and missing-country selection, and quantify the resulting rank sensitivity. Do not add unsupported precision or reinterpret this exploratory index as absolute attention.

Browser-refinement references were chosen from observed series peaks, using a known topic with lower measured search interest where practical. Removing other strong topics from the batch can also improve the scale available to a target. This changes measurement precision, not the underlying definition of attention. No value was inferred from population, GDP, neighboring countries or a different search platform. After the refinements, Cabo Verde's original-score range is 1–100; Comoros and Haiti each have a paired export ranging from 4–100. Their own peaks already use the top of the scale, so merely weakening the other topic cannot remove the remaining whole-window rounding limitation. Their primary per-capita ranks are withheld. Repeated exports can differ because Google samples searches; the current panel does not estimate that sampling distribution.

A subsequent retry downloaded paired comparisons for all three. Cabo Verde remained at 1–100; Haiti and Comoros returned byte-identical CSVs to the previous paired exports. The new Cabo Verde comparison is `browser-075`. Identical repeats were recorded under `data/raw/followup_checks/2026-10-16/` and were not added to the calibration graph or treated as independent sampling evidence. Collecting shorter windows would require a separately validated method for linking resolutions and windows; the current estimator requires matching windows and resolution, so those fragments are not combined into the published annual index.


## Why these comparisons were chosen

The research question is how worldwide Google country-topic interest compares with the size of the country being searched for. The population denominator is a chosen benchmark, not an empirically established entitlement to attention. An index proportional to population would give equal per-resident scores; smaller countries can score highly without having a large total audience. The regression is a separate descriptive benchmark that allows the association with population and GDP to vary. It does not certify that the remaining attention is deserved, excessive, international, or caused by any omitted factor.

Topics are preferred to English names because Google groups concept-related terms across languages. Reviewing entity labels prevents obvious identity mistakes but cannot audit Google's proprietary classification. Domestic searches remain in the worldwide numerator, and different patterns of Google use across search origins are part of this measurement. Neither population division nor GDP adjustment corrects those coverage differences. Vatican City, Taiwan and Palestine retain their territorial and denominator qualifications described above. Do not interpret the index as all global attention, cultural influence, unique audiences, or foreign interest.

The shared-reference graph addresses a specific problem: each comparison has its own 0–100 multiplier. Median log ratios limit the influence of occasional mismatches; weak references improve integer precision. Link weights count usable overlaps, but correlated weeks and links sharing the same export are not independent observations. The six-overlap and 0.2 log-deviation rules are engineering diagnostics, not estimated error probabilities. A connected graph with small residuals can still share systematic measurement error. Synthetic tests check the implementation and cannot validate Google's measurements.

Equal-month weighting gives each calendar month the same role in the annual index. It avoids interpreting an average search share as an additive count. Day weighting within each month allocates weekly observations consistently, while assuming the weekly share is constant over the covered days. That approximation can move a short spike between adjacent months. Peak-month removal tests sensitivity to concentration, rather than identifying an event or removing inconvenient observations.

## Deterministic sensitivity checks

Every offline build writes `ranking_sensitivity.csv` and `sensitivity.json`. The primary eligibility rule and per-capita ranks remain unchanged. Scenario ranks always use the full primary eligible cohort, including separately identified additional entities; they are not ranks within a filtered web-page subset. Each country receives a minimum and maximum observed scenario rank and the number of available scenarios, including the primary calculation. The file also retains each individual scenario rank.

The scenarios are specified before examining their results:

1. Replace highest-original-score selection with the median of calibrated observations whose original scores are at least 5, for each country and interval. Where none qualify, retain the original selected observation and its low-precision status. This checks dependence on the duplicate-measurement selection rule without adding repeat observations as traffic.
2. Fit the same accepted calibration links with equal weight instead of overlap-count weight. Keep the primary residual tolerance; a failed fit is reported as unavailable. This checks whether counting correlated overlaps changes the result materially.
3. In turn, exclude each country topic appearing in at least three batches and contributing to accepted calibration links from link estimation, while retaining its measured series. Refit the graph with the original gates. A disconnected or inconsistent graph is recorded as an unavailable scenario with a reason; it is not counted as a stable result. These omissions test dependence on widely reused references. Merely changing France to another final reference would multiply every value by the same constant and cannot test rank robustness.

The resulting minimum and maximum ranks describe only these choices on these exports. They are not a probability interval, do not encompass all plausible estimators, and must not be labelled a 95% confidence interval. A country cannot be called robust merely because the checks that would disconnect its reference path are unavailable. Near ties should be described as similar scores unless fresh sampling and calibration checks support their ordering. Broad qualitative groups may be useful, but the project does not fabricate statistical equivalence groups from a single panel.

## Annual precision diagnostics

The current eligibility gate rejects any selected score below 5. This prevents quiet weeks with coarse integer precision from entering the primary ranking, but can penalize a country with a single sharp peak. A score of 4 represents relative scaling within an export, not a known number of searches. The rule does not establish that a country had too few searches, or that its annual estimate is necessarily unusable.

To assess this distinction, `ranking_sensitivity.csv` records the number of low-score intervals and their fraction of the annual unnormalized signal, using exactly the primary equal-month weighting. Thus one week near a month edge is weighted by its covered days in each month; this diagnostic is not an unweighted weekly average. It remains missing for censored or incomplete years. These values are available for excluded countries without assigning them a primary rank.

For an uncensored integer score y, the rounding diagnostic uses the conservative interval `[max(0, y - 0.5), min(100, y + 0.5)]`. Multiply its endpoints by the fitted batch scale and aggregate them with the same calendar weights. If L_i and U_i are the resulting annual endpoints, the attention envelope is `[100 L_i / U_ref, 100 U_i / L_ref]`; the reference country's index is exactly 100 by definition. Rank envelopes compare L_i/P_i and U_i/P_i directly because the common reference multiplier cancels. The best possible rank counts other eligible countries whose lower endpoint exceeds this country's upper endpoint; the worst counts those whose upper endpoint exceeds its lower endpoint. Ties use the minimum-rank convention.

These are rounding-only envelopes conditional on fixed fitted calibration scales, the chosen observations, fixed populations and constant shares inside each source interval. They omit Google sampling, privacy noise, calibration error, population uncertainty and topic classification error. They must not be used as full uncertainty intervals or as an automatic admission rule. A narrow envelope can coexist with substantial unmeasured error.

Replacing the every-week gate requires an empirical validation study. Before collecting that study, specify the intended use (for example broad comparisons or top-20 membership), the tolerable annual error and rank instability, and a collection protocol. Test a proposed annual criterion against separately collected panels and changed reference links, retain missingness and failures, and report how inclusion changes the country mix. Do not choose the tolerance after seeing which countries it admits. The current implementation deliberately reports diagnostics without silently adopting an unvalidated threshold.

## Repeated panels and publication standard

The original 76 exports were collected on 16 October 2026. They are linked comparisons within one panel, not 76 independent replications. A later export can differ because of sampling, noise or source revisions; a new date or hash alone does not establish independence. Identical downloads do not add sampling evidence. Repeated observations from the same cache or sample should not be counted as independent draws, and bootstrapping the existing weeks does not recreate Google's sampling process.

The collection guide specifies how to preserve separate panels and run `compare-panels`. That command verifies matching windows, search settings and country-topic identities, rejects a repeat made entirely of previously seen export hashes, reports partial hash reuse, and recalibrates each panel independently. It holds primary populations fixed so demographic revisions cannot masquerade as search instability. Rank ranges use only countries eligible in every compared panel; countries excluded from this comparison are listed explicitly. These observed repeat-panel ranges are descriptive, not calibrated sampling confidence intervals.

Publication as a validated ranking requires repeated collection on separate dates under a fixed design, evidence about cached or reused samples, successful alternate-reference comparisons where feasible, and sensitivity to annual precision and denominator choices. Report sample count, dates, missing countries, failed calibration checks and both score and rank variation. Model HC3 intervals address regression error conditional on the supplied inputs; they do not propagate search measurement uncertainty. Keep current-dollar GDP, possible specification differences and territorial/population definitions visible when interpreting adjusted ranks. Exploratory publication should retain these caveats next to the results.

It covers all 197 topics; none of its original-export hashes match the first panel. Both panels pass the unchanged calibration checks.
